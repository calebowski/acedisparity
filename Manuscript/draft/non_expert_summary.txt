Palaeontologists compare the diversity of shape and sizes of animals to understand how life evolved. However, the fossil record is patchy and lots of data is missing. One solution is to estimate what these missing ancestors may have looked like based on the evolutionary relationships between the descendent species we have data for. In this paper we tested methods for this process, using computer-generated data where the true evolutionary history is known. We found that including these estimated ancestors usually improved our recovery of the true diversity, but the best method depended on how much data we had and the process of evolution. Our results provide guidance for researchers who are deciding which method to use when completing these analyses.
